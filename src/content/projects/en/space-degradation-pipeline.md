## Problem

Lifetime assessment for space photovoltaics rests on irradiation experiments — beam time, handling, and a long wait per data point. Screening a materials space that way is not affordable, so candidate selection happens on terrestrial intuition and gets checked far too late.

## Approach

A chain that carries a mission specification through to a lifetime number, with each stage handing a physical quantity to the next rather than a fitted proxy:

**SPENVIS** defines the orbital particle environment → **SRIM** gives displacement damage from the resulting proton spectrum → **SR-NIEL** converts that to non-ionizing energy loss and displacement damage dose → **SCAPS-1D** turns the damage profile into device-level electrical behaviour → a Python ML layer maps that to a lifetime label.

The synthetic datasets carry NIEL, DDD, vacancy depth profiles and space-T_S80 labels across twenty-nine space-environment variables, generated by a three-exponential degradation model that separates burn-in, light-soaking and long-term decay, with self-healing factors and shallow and deep trap populations kept apart rather than lumped into one trap density.
